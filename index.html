Are Ireland's data centres driving electricity prices?

A high correlation makes a compelling chart. It does not make a causal result. This project tests what survives once gas, wholesale electricity, time, lags and structural breaks enter the analysis.

01 / THE TRAP

Two rising lines can tell the wrong story.

Data-centre demand rose rapidly. Electricity prices also rose. The raw relationship is strong—but it changes materially across regimes and transformations.

04 / ANSWER

The simple headline does not survive the tests.

The evidence does not support the claim that rising data-centre electricity use directly caused Ireland's observed business-electricity price surge.

The 2021–2023 shock is much more directly associated with gas and wholesale electricity. Data-centre growth may contribute to longer-run infrastructure requirements, but its specific cost share cannot be identified from the public data used here.

Strong
Short-run retail-price shock is primarily energy-market driven in this dataset.
Does adding DC demand improve out-of-sample price prediction?
Weak / mixed
No robust incremental predictive evidence from the available small sample.
Could DC growth contribute indirectly through network/capacity requirements?
Moderate mechanism / weak attribution
Network-cost channel should be described as plausible, not quantified causally.
