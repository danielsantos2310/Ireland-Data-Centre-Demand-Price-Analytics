# Project 03 — Final analytical conclusion

## Headline question

> **Are Ireland's data centres driving electricity prices?**

## Evidence-based answer

**Not in the simple direct sense implied by the headline.**

Ireland's data-centre electricity demand has increased dramatically, reaching roughly 23% of metered electricity consumption in 2025. Over the full 2015–2025 period, data-centre demand and business electricity-price levels are strongly correlated.

However, that raw correlation is not stable enough to support a causal claim.

When the analysis moves from trending levels to year-on-year growth, first differences, regime splits, lag tests and controlled walk-forward models, the relationship weakens substantially. Gas and wholesale electricity variables explain the 2021–2023 retail-price shock much more directly. Adding data-centre demand does not generate robust incremental out-of-sample predictive value across the tested specifications.

The strongest defensible interpretation is therefore:

> **The observed Irish business-electricity price surge was primarily an energy-market shock, not a simple consequence of rising data-centre electricity consumption.**

That does **not** mean data-centre growth has zero cost impact.

A separate and more plausible channel is long-run network and capacity investment. Large-load growth contributes to future infrastructure requirements alongside renewable integration, electrification, housing, business growth, resilience and security-of-supply needs. CRU's PR6 programme confirms that Ireland is entering a period of unusually large network investment, but currently available public data do not provide a defensible allocation of those network costs specifically to data centres.

Therefore Project 03 distinguishes two statements:

1. **Direct short-run price claim:** not supported robustly by this dataset.
2. **Indirect long-run system-cost contribution:** plausible, but not separately quantifiable from the public data used here.

## What Project 03 does not claim

- It does not estimate the private tariff paid by individual data centres.
- It does not claim Band IG is a data-centre tariff.
- It does not infer causality from correlation.
- It does not estimate a data-centre-specific share of PR6 network investment.
- It does not claim absence of evidence equals evidence of zero effect.

## Forward exposure, not causation

Combining Project 01's data-centre demand forecast with Project 02's **Band IG benchmark price scenarios** gives a transparent expenditure sensitivity.

This is **not a tariff forecast** and **not a causal cost allocation**. It simply answers:

> If projected data-centre electricity consumption were valued at the public Band IG benchmark scenario price, what scale of annual electricity expenditure would that imply?

By 2035, Project 01 forecasts approximately **14.31 TWh** of annual data-centre electricity consumption.

Under Project 02's real-2025-money Band IG scenarios, 2035 benchmark expenditure exposure is approximately:

- **Low-cost transition:** €2.01bn/year
- **Central:** €2.32bn/year
- **High-cost / constrained:** €2.90bn/year

The purpose is to quantify scale and sensitivity—not to infer actual procurement costs.
